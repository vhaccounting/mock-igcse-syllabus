Written question · 5 marks

Discuss whether the business should continue to prepare a monthly bank reconciliation statement.

Say this for

It confirms the cash book agrees with the bank statement, so the cash at bank figure in the statement of financial position can be trusted.

Say this against

Doing it every month takes time, so a small business owner could spend that time trading instead, especially as online banking shows the balance any time.

Finish with

He should continue, because it is his main check against error and fraud in the cash book.

Written question · 5 marks

Discuss whether the business should continue to use the imprest system for its petty cash book.

Say this for

The float is restored to a fixed amount each period, so the cash at risk is limited and cash in hand plus vouchers must always equal the float — a clear check on the petty cashier.

Say this against

If the float is set too low the cashier runs out of money early, so she must ask for extra cash, which creates more paperwork.

Finish with

She should keep the imprest system, as long as the float is reviewed and set at a sensible amount.

Written question · 5 marks

Discuss whether the business should change from the reducing balance method to the straight-line method.

Say this for

Straight line spreads the cost evenly and is simpler to work out, so it suits an asset used at about the same rate every year.

Say this against

Changing method breaks the consistency concept, and a vehicle loses most value in its early years, so reducing balance matches the charge to the benefit received.

Finish with

She should not change, unless the way the asset is used has genuinely changed — and then the change must be disclosed.

Written question · 5 marks

Explain, referring to a relevant accounting concept, whether the owner needs to keep a record of goods or cash taken for personal use.

Name the concept

Not recording them breaks the business entity concept, which says the business is completely separate from its owner.

Give the effect

Without a record, cost of materials used and cost of sales are too high, so production cost and gross profit are too low and the statements are not true and fair.

Finish with

He should keep a full record of all drawings.

02
Adjustments
25 marks
Full marks expected
▾

What you must prepareIncome statement (profit and loss section) · Statement of financial position

Format BookPages 6–7

Income statement, profit and loss section.

Format BookPages 5 & 21

Statement of financial position.

Theory BookPage 20

Accruals, prepayments and the provision for irrecoverable debts.

Expense = Paid + owing at end − owing at start
Expense = Paid − prepaid at end + prepaid at start
Provision = Receivables after write-off × %
Watch outOnly the change in the provision goes in the income statement — the full provision goes in the statement of financial position. If your totals do not balance, you lose the last mark.
Written question · 5 marks

Advise, referring to two accounting concepts, whether closing inventory should be valued at selling price instead of cost.

First concept

It would break prudence, which says profit must not be overstated and assets must not be valued above what can be recovered.

Second concept

It would also break realisation, because profit would be counted before the goods were actually sold.

Finish with

She should not do it. Inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value.

How written answers score

Two points for, two points against, one conclusion that picks a side. Every point must carry on with "…so…" or "…which means…" — one-word answers score half.
