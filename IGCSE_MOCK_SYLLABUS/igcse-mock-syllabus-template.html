Written question · 2 marks

State two reasons why a trade receivables control account may have a credit balance.

Any two of these
  • A customer has overpaid the amount owed.
  • A customer has paid in advance, or a deposit has been received.
  • Goods were returned after the account had already been settled.
  • A credit note was issued after the customer had paid.
Written question · 5 marks

Evaluate whether the business should continue to prepare control accounts now that a computerised accounting system has been installed.

Say this for

A control account gives an independent check on the trade receivables ledger, so errors and possible fraud can be found, because the totals are built up from a different source.

Say this against

On a computer, the control account and the personal accounts are both posted from the same data entry, so the account will always agree and the arithmetical check becomes largely meaningless.

Finish with

She should continue preparing them, because they still give a fast total for trade receivables for the financial statements — even though the checking function is now reduced.

Careful

Points listed without development score a maximum of 2. The last mark needs a decision, not a repeat of what you already said.

Provision for depreciation account, disposal account and the formulas.

Reducing balance = Carrying value × %

Watch outTwo vehicles here, bought in different years, and one is sold. No depreciation is charged in the year of sale, so this year's charge covers the remaining vehicle only. Work out each vehicle separately on a rough page before you write anything.

Suspense & Correction of Errors
15 marks
▾

What you must doWrite journal entries to correct four errors, prepare the suspense account, then name three errors a trial balance cannot find.

Theory BookPage 22

The six errors a trial balance cannot find, and the errors that make it disagree.

Format BookPages 1–2

Journal layout and the suspense account.

Ask of every error: were both a debit and a credit made?
Yes → no suspense entry. No → suspense is used.
Written question · 3 marks

State three types of error that do not affect the agreement of a trial balance.

Any three of these
  • Error of omission — the transaction was left out completely.
  • Error of commission — right type of account, wrong person's account.
  • Error of principle — completely the wrong type of account.
  • Error of original entry — the wrong amount used on both sides.
  • Compensating error — two errors of the same size cancel out.
  • Complete reversal — debit and credit the wrong way round.

Adjustments — Accruals & Prepayments
15 marks
Full marks expected
▾

What you must doPrepare the rent payable account and the insurance account, then explain the concept behind them.

Theory BookPage 20

Accruals and prepayments — expense owing and expense paid in advance.

Theory BookPages 15–17

The accruals and matching concept.

Format BookPages 6–7

Expense accounts with accruals and prepayments.

Charge = Paid + owing at end − owing at start
Charge = Paid − prepaid at end + prepaid at start
Watch outAn amount owing at the start is a credit balance b/d; an amount prepaid at the start is a debit balance b/d. Get those two the wrong way round and the whole account is wrong. The figure going to the income statement is the balancing figure, not the amount paid.
Written question · 4 marks

Explain, referring to a relevant accounting concept, why the amount transferred to the income statement for rent payable differs from the amount paid during the year.

Name the concept

This is the accruals, or matching, concept, which says that expenses belong to the period they relate to, not the period in which the cash happens to be paid.

Apply it to the figures

Part of the amount paid this year settled rent owed from the previous year, and part of it paid for the year ahead in advance. Both are taken out, so the charge is reduced to the rent that genuinely belongs to this year.

Finish with

The prepayment is carried forward instead as a current asset in the statement of financial position.

Manufacturing Business
25 marks
▾

What you must prepareManufacturing account · Income statement (trading section)

Format BookPage 8

Manufacturing account.

Format BookPages 4 & 9

Income statement, trading section.

Theory BookPage 28

Direct and indirect costs, and how shared costs are split.

Watch outPurchases of finished goods go in the income statement, not the manufacturing account. Putting it in the manufacturing account is a recurring national error and loses the mark outright. The labels prime cost and cost of production must actually be written — no label, no mark. Office shares of rent and insurance stay out. Carriage inwards on raw materials is added to purchases.
Written question · 5 marks

Discuss whether the business should continue to manufacture its goods rather than buy them ready-made from a supplier.

Say this for

Manufacturing lets him control the quality and specification of the goods directly, and his own cost of production may well be lower than the price a supplier would charge, which protects his gross profit.

Say this against

Manufacturing ties up money in machinery, raw materials and work in progress, and the fixed factory overheads must be paid whether output is high or low.

Finish with

He should keep manufacturing as long as output stays high enough to absorb those overheads. If demand fell a lot, buying ready-made would be the safer option.

Top tip

Quote the actual figures from your own answer — the cost of production and the total factory overheads. Real numbers turn an identification mark into a development mark.

02
Incomplete Records
25 marks
Biggest question on the paper
▾

What you must prepareOpening capital · Credit sales · Full income statement · A written evaluation of two ratios

Theory BookPage __

Incomplete records — finding missing figures.

Format BookPage __

Statement of affairs and the control account method.

Format BookPages 4 & 6–7

Income statement.

Theory BookPage __

Profitability ratios.

Capital = Assets − Liabilities
Credit sales: build a receivables control account and find the missing figure
Total revenue = Credit sales + cash sales − sales returns
Expense = Paid + owing at end − owing at start
Watch outWrite "Profit for the year", never "net profit" — the old term is marked wrong. The labels Revenue, Gross profit and Profit for the year must all appear. Three separate calculations feed into the income statement, so an early mistake follows you through — but own-figure marks mean you still score if the method is right, so show every working.
Written question · 5 marks

Evaluate the change in the profitability of the business over the two years.

What got worse

Gross profit as a percentage of revenue fell from 44.20% to 41.21%, so every $100 of sales now leaves about $3 less gross profit — the likely causes are selling prices being cut, suppliers charging more, or goods being lost or stolen.

What was not as bad

Profit for the year fell by less than gross profit did, from 15.80% to 13.28%, so the fall was caused at the trading stage, not by overheads — she has actually controlled her expenses slightly better relative to sales.

Finish with

Profitability has got worse overall, so she should investigate her purchase prices and her mark-up before she starts trying to cut expenses.

Careful

Just saying the ratios went down scores a maximum of 2. Every movement needs a reason attached to it.

How written answers score

Two points for, two points against, one conclusion that picks a side. Every point must carry on with "…so…" or "…which means…" — one-word answers score half.
